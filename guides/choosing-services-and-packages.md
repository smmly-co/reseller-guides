# Choosing Social Media Services and Building Packages

A useful service package starts with a customer need. Do not begin by copying a competitor's catalogue or combining unrelated services into a large bundle.

## 1. Pick one customer problem

Examples:

- A local business needs consistent content.
- A creator needs short-form video editing.
- A brand needs campaign planning and reporting.
- A small agency needs help with publishing operations.
- A reseller needs a defined catalogue and order workflow.

## 2. Separate service types

Your offer may include one or more of these categories:

- **Strategy:** audits, positioning, content plans and campaign recommendations.
- **Production:** design, copywriting, photography and video editing.
- **Publishing:** scheduling, platform formatting and content management.
- **Community:** moderation and response workflows.
- **Advertising:** campaign setup, monitoring and reporting.
- **Catalogue-based services:** specific services purchased through a provider or panel, subject to its terms.

Do not describe a catalogue-based service as strategy, creative work or guaranteed business growth if it is not.

## 3. Define packages by deliverables

A package should say exactly what the client receives.

For example:

**Monthly content support**
- 8 short-form video edits from client-supplied footage.
- 8 captions.
- A monthly content calendar.
- One performance review.
- One revision round per asset.

This is clearer than saying "full Instagram growth package."

## 4. Set boundaries

State what is excluded, such as paid advertising budget, original filming, additional revisions, influencer fees or customer support outside agreed hours.

## 5. Review before expanding

Start with a service you can fulfil reliably. Gather feedback and improve the process before adding more deliverables.
